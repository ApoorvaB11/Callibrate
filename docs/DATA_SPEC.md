# Callibrate — synthetic data specification

Last updated 7 Nov 2026 · Product owner: Apoorva Bhardwaj

Callibrate's data is generated by `generator/generate.py` from one configuration file (`generator/config.yaml`) and one random seed, so the same command always rebuilds the same data. It contains no real doctor, rep, company, brand, patient or prescription. This document explains what the generator builds and how; [ANSWER_KEY.md](ANSWER_KEY.md) lists the planted scenarios, and [CALIBRATION.md](CALIBRATION.md) shows how close the data lands to its targets.

## How to rebuild the data

```bash
pip install duckdb numpy pandas pyyaml scikit-learn faker zipcodes
python generator/generate.py     # about 30 seconds; writes data/callibrate.duckdb (about 120 MB)
python generator/calibrate.py    # checks the data and rewrites docs/CALIBRATION.md
```

The database file is not stored in the repository; anyone can rebuild it with the two commands above.

**Time.** Weeks run Saturday to Friday. Weeks 1–104 (2 Nov 2024 to 30 Oct 2026) are the reporting period. Weeks −51 to 0 are history, kept so new-to-brand prescriptions can look back 52 weeks, as industry prescription audits do.

## How the data is generated

The generator works like a small simulation of a US market, in nine steps. Prescribing is driven by hidden doctor traits and noise, never by the app's priority score or lapsing rule, so the app's analytics have to find patterns rather than replay their own formulas (FRD FR-91).

1. **Geography.** Real ZIP codes in Florida, Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi and Louisiana form two regions (Southeast, Gulf) and five districts. Only ZIP, city, county and coordinates are used; there are no street addresses.
2. **Doctors.** 10,000 doctors, 2,000 per district. Specialty counts are set so each specialty's share of GLP-1 volume matches the BRD prescriber mix. Each doctor gets a hidden practice volume (a lognormal draw times a specialty factor); 8% write no GLP-1 prescriptions at all.
3. **Territories.** Doctors in each district are grouped into 8 compact territories of 250 doctors each; every ZIP is aligned to its nearest territory.
4. **Reps and call plan.** One rep per territory. Each rep targets 150 doctors ranked by a noisy view of last year's volume: 25 A, 55 B and 70 C, planned for 6, 3 and 1 calls a quarter.
5. **Calls.** Each rep executes 72–95% of planned calls, less for limited-access and no-see doctors, then fills the rest of the week with extra calls on favourite targets and about 8% on non-targets. Channel depends on access tier. Targets also receive emails.
6. **Payers.** Each new patient gets a line of business (45% Medicare, 40% commercial, 15% Medicaid) and a plan available in their region. Each plan sets a formulary status for each brand: preferred, preferred with prior authorisation, or non-preferred with prior authorisation.
7. **New prescriptions.** Each doctor writes new GLP-1 prescriptions at their hidden rate. The brand is chosen from the doctor's own brand preferences, lifted for Glyvexa by recent calls (with diminishing returns) and a slow launch trend. 22% of prescriptions are never taken to a pharmacy.
8. **Pharmacy adjudication.** A first fill under prior authorisation can be rejected with NCPDP reject code 75. After a rejection the patient is approved later, abandons therapy, or switches to a competitor, with chances set by the formulary status.
9. **Refills.** Paid patients refill every 4 weeks until they stop; the chance of stopping after each fill differs by line of business, giving about 60% persistence at 12 months. A new Rx number starts about once a year, which counts as a new prescription (NRx) but not as new to brand (NBRx).

## Identifiers and privacy

- Doctor IDs are 10 digits starting with 1 or 2, like NPIs, but every one deliberately fails the NPI check digit; the calibration report confirms none passes.
- Doctor, rep and patient names are generated by the Faker library. Patient IDs are sequential codes.
- Public data enters only as aggregate targets written into `config.yaml` (specialty volume mix, access level, persistence and so on). No public file with real identifiers is read by the generator or stored in the repository.

## Limitations

- Calibration targets come from published aggregates and vendor benchmarks, several of them low-confidence (see the BRD realism defaults).
- Medicare Part D data, the main public source of prescribing patterns, covers Medicare only and hides low-volume prescribers.
- Field-force behaviour (calls per day, adherence, channel mix) is modelled, not observed.
- Payments to doctors and speaker programmes are not modelled.
- Everything the app finds in this data was planted or emerges from the stated rules; it proves the analysis works, not that the same patterns exist in a real market.
